21.1 The concept of congruent triangles

Two triangles are congruent (全等) if one can be moved — by translation, rotation and/or reflection (flipping) — so that it coincides (完全重疊) exactly with the other.

If △ABC ≅ △PQR, then

  • AB = PQ, BC = QR, CA = RP (corr. sides, ≅△s)
  • ∠A = ∠P, ∠B = ∠Q, ∠C = ∠R (corr. ∠s, ≅△s)
Order matters! In “△ABC ≅ △PQR”, the 1st, 2nd and 3rd letters are the corresponding vertices.

If any pair of corresponding sides or angles is not equal, the triangles are not congruent.

21.3 Property of isosceles triangles 等腰三角形的性質

In an isosceles triangle, the two equal sides are the legs; the angles opposite them are the base angles.

If AB = AC, then ∠B = ∠C. (base ∠s, isos. △)

Drag the red dot. Watch AB, AC, ∠B and ∠C. What happens when AB = AC?

Proof (SAS) · “Pick up, flip over and put back”

Compare the triangle with itself, reading the vertices in a different order: △ABC and △ACB.

  1. In △ABC and △ACB,
  2. AB = ACgiven
  3. ∠BAC = ∠CABcommon angle
  4. AC = ABgiven
  5. ∴ △ABC ≅ △ACBSAS
  6. ∴ ∠ABC = ∠ACBcorr. ∠s, ≅△s
Teacher's note The curriculum suggests proving this property by SAS (△ABC ≅ △ACB). Avoid joining A to the mid-point of BC: the proof of SSS and the compass-and-straightedge constructions (angle bisector, perpendicular bisector, perpendicular lines) depend on this property, so that would be a circular argument.

21.4 Condition for isosceles triangles 等腰三角形的判定

If ∠B = ∠C, then AB = AC. (sides opp. eq. ∠s)

This is the converse of 21.3: if two angles of a triangle are equal, the sides opposite them are equal, so the triangle is isosceles.

Explore · Change the base angles

Proof (ASA)

  1. In △ABC and △ACB,
  2. ∠ABC = ∠ACBgiven
  3. BC = CBcommon side
  4. ∠ACB = ∠ABCgiven
  5. ∴ △ABC ≅ △ACBASA
  6. ∴ AB = ACcorr. sides, ≅△s

Equilateral triangles = Equiangular triangles

Equilateral ⇒ equiangular
AB = AC ⇒ ∠B = ∠C; BA = BC ⇒ ∠A = ∠C (base ∠s, isos. △)
So ∠A = ∠B = ∠C = 180° ÷ 3 = 60°.
Equiangular ⇒ equilateral
∠B = ∠C ⇒ AB = AC; ∠A = ∠C ⇒ BA = BC (sides opp. eq. ∠s)
So AB = BC = CA.

For triangles, “regular triangle”, “equilateral triangle” and “equiangular triangle” all mean the same kind of triangle (link with LO 20.1 Regular polygons).
